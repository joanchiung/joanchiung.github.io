## CEX: the crypto version of an "exchange plus brokerage", taking a cut at every step

A CEX, a centralized exchange, is a combination of a traditional exchange and a brokerage, but with far weaker regulation. It plays both roles at once: it lets projects pay to list a token (like an exchange), and it executes trades for users and takes a commission (like a broker).

Concretely, a CEX earns from listing fees paid by projects, a fee on every user trade, a withdrawal fee, and a cut of its yield products. Every single thing you do on a CEX, the exchange takes a slice.

## DEX: no company in the middle, the money flows to whoever provides liquidity

A DEX, a decentralized exchange, has no central company matching trades. It runs on smart contracts. Anyone who wants to trade interacts directly with a liquidity pool. The money in that pool comes from a group of people who voluntarily deposit funds (LPs, liquidity providers), and most of the fee on each trade flows back to those LPs, with a small part going into the protocol's treasury. The DEX company itself earns from an extra fee on its web interface, plus the large block of governance tokens it held early on.

## Who holds your money? That is the fundamental difference

- A CEX custodies your assets for you. Your coins sit on the exchange's books, which means you are trusting this company not to misuse your money.
- A DEX custodies nothing. The LPs' money sits in an on-chain pool, and a trader's money is in their own wallet from start to finish. So even if the DEX's official website goes dark, the smart contract on-chain keeps running and most people's funds are unaffected.

## The risks are different: a CEX fears human nature, a DEX fears its own code

- The CEX risk is that the rules are not tight enough, and without an enforced requirement to segregate client funds, the company has room to quietly move customer assets into risky bets. That is how FTX collapsed; users went through years of legal proceedings to recover part of their money, with no insurance to cover the rest.
- The DEX risk is that the smart contract may have a bug. Once a hacker finds it, the money in the pool can be drained in an instant, again with no insurance and no way to recover the loss.
